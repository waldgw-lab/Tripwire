name: Claims organization
description: Article-focus-first Claims organization, primary homes and exact source linkages.
---
Use the approved twelve development categories as the primary organization for
Claims, with related topic threads inside each category. The user specifically
wants Hegseth-related staffing reporting grouped separately from the Stephen
Miller / Susie Wiles staffing transition.

**Why:** The user wants to make sense of Claims quickly, not scan a long flat list
of extracted statements.

**How to apply:** Keep the overview compact, expose category/topic filtering and
sorting, and show a feed article's actual claim linkages inside its detail view
rather than adding noise to feed rows. Topic grouping is navigation, not permission
to merge claim evidence, assume independent confirmation, resolve anonymous
identities or assert who holds an office. Retain ambiguous material for review.

Each topic must have one primary category. Applicable secondary categories belong
as tags in the item's details, not as duplicate topic groups in other areas.

**Why:** The user does not want related items split across multiple category areas.

**How to apply:** Assign the topic's home before filtering the overview. Show
secondary context in Claims and the feed item's linked-claims details.
Mirror preview counts and date ranges in the corresponding detail scope. Keep
secondary topic tags and related subjects collapsed below Developments.

**Why:** The user wants the preview information available in detail without
letting secondary context dominate the reporting.

**How to apply:** Keep summary/detail counts in the same reporting scope and make
secondary context available on demand beneath the development summary.

Keep operational extraction/processing information on Dashboard, not in the
Claims header area. That space is for linked noteworthy reporting developments
from the last 12 hours, when such developments exist.

**Why:** The user wants Claims to lead with relevant developments rather than
processing status.

**How to apply:** Keep processing history and recovery controls accessible on
Dashboard. Use publication times for the alert window and exact article or
focus navigation; recent ingestion alone is not a new development.

Group first by article focus or larger category, then characterize that grouping
by its developments. Do not let an individual development steer the grouping.

**Why:** The user explicitly corrected the development-first recommendation.

**How to apply:** Use the main focus of the linked articles rather than isolated
claim verbs or incidental names to establish a group's home. Related developments
such as impeachment efforts, possible removal and leadership confidence can
describe the same focus group without splitting its articles into action-based
groups. Keep claims beneath their reporting articles, with secondary categories
and background context in the details.

A shared proposition may be reported by articles with different primary focuses.
That does not give any one article several primary homes.

**Why:** The user requires article-focus-first organization while retaining the
actual reporting and claims beneath each article.

**How to apply:** Preserve the same claim identity and all exact source links,
but count and display only the articles actually assigned to each focus group.
Do not move all of a shared claim's source articles into its representative
category, or duplicate claim records to make navigation work.

Measure subtopic coverage primarily by distinct reporting articles, and group
each article's claims beneath that article rather than presenting a flat list.
Keep statement counts secondary and explicitly labeled.

**Why:** The user wants to know how many articles report on a subtopic; multiple
claims extracted from one article must not look like multiple reports.

**How to apply:** Use exact article linkages and distinct article identities for
coverage totals, not statement/capture counts or sums that double-count shared
articles. Keep claims without article attributions separate. An article count
still does not establish independent sourcing or confirmation.

Selecting a focus group must show its reporting articles together, not silently
substitute the evidence view for an automatically selected individual claim.
Keep individual-claim coverage distinct and provide a route back to the group.

**Why:** The user saw a six-article focus count alongside one claim's single
article and reasonably thought the other reporting was missing.

**How to apply:** Treat group overview and individual claim evidence as separate
navigation states; preserve that distinction through reload and browser history.

Analyst focus corrections are article-local, including their readable labels.
Choosing an existing focus joins that home; changing a label does not rename
the shared group or silently move its other reporting.

**Why:** Unusual stories need correction without changing unrelated articles,
claim identities or sourcing judgments.

**How to apply:** Keep shared-group management separate from article correction.
Clearing a correction returns only that article to current automatic placement.

Shared-group label/category edits are a separate, explicitly confirmed action
with an affected-article preview; resetting the shared correction leaves
article-only choices intact.

**Why:** The user approved expanding Claims editing beyond article placement
while keeping the scope of changes understandable.

**How to apply:** Preserve the group identity when renaming or changing its
primary category. Do not silently turn an article edit into a bulk action.

Correcting a passage's claim association must keep its original quotation,
capture, offsets and previous associations. Prior sourcing reviews must not
automatically establish independent support for the newly linked claim.

**Why:** Organization and analyst corrections are not new reporting or verified
independence.

**How to apply:** Use append-only association history and active projections;
require a fresh sourcing assessment after an association changes.
